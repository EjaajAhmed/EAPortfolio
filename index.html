About Me

I'm a software engineer at CanWorks and the founder of Venue Usher.

Venue Usher is a chat assistant for wedding and event venues. It answers website inquiries the moment they come in, day or night, in the language the couple writes in, and hands every lead to the venue's team. The idea came from four years of setting up weddings and events at Casa Loma, where I learned how venues actually run. Since launching in November I've built demos for over 50 venues, earned a CA$500 investor grant and taken the product to venues across the GTA by phone, email and in person.

At CanWorks I work on Pathfinder, a job-matching platform for Canadian university students. I built its two-factor authentication, led a zero-downtime cloud migration that cut hosting costs 44% and led AI workshops that teach students outside engineering how to build with AI agents.

I also build things people actually use. DidTheyHockey, my NHL trivia game, draws around 3,000 visitors a month.

My first language was Java. In grade 5 I tried to finish the Khan Academy Java course and didn't come close. Years later a university web development course taught me that Java and JavaScript are different languages, which nearly gave me an aneurysm, and I've been building for the web ever since. I graduated from York University's Lassonde School of Engineering in 2026 with a degree in Computer Science.

Outside of code I'm a huge sports fan. I also enjoy reading, biking and learning languages, and my French is at a B1 level.
